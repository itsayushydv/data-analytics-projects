UK E-Commerce Dataset — Final Report Analysis

Prepared by: Ayush | Data Analyst Dataset: Kaggle — carrie1/ecommerce-data UK-based online retailer, Dec 2010–Dec 2011, 541,909 transaction line items

Executive Summary

This report analyzes 13 months of transactional data from a UK-based online retailer specializing in unique all-occasion gifts, sold primarily to wholesale customers.
After cleaning, the dataset yields 524,878 valid sales line itemsacross 4,338 identified customers, ~4,000 products, and 38 countries.
Top-line findings:
  1. Revenue is heavily concentrated. The top ~26% of customers generate 80% of revenue, and a single RFM segment ("Champions") — 26% of customers — accounts for 66.5% of total revenue
  2. Retention is weak and flat. Only ~21–24% of customers in any acquisition cohort return to purchase again within 6 months, and this doesn't meaningfully improve over time — suggesting a one-and-done pattern rather than habitual repeat buying.
  3. The business is wholesale/B2B, not consumer retail. No Saturday trading at all, revenue concentrated on weekdays during business hours, and a median order value of £303 point to bulk-buying business customers rather than individual shoppers.
  4. Growth is UK-dependent (84.6% of revenue) with the Netherlands, EIRE, and Germany as the largest — but still minor — international markets.
  5. Revenue is strongly seasonal, peaking in November ahead of the holiday season, which has direct implications for inventory and staffing planning.
This report consolidates four supporting notebooks: Data cleaning RFM Segmentation Cohort Analysis and Revenue Trend. Each contains the full methodology, code, and intermediate diagnostics behind the summaries below.

Data Overview and cleaning

Key cleaning decisions:
  • Cancelled orders (InvoiceNo starting with 'C') were separated out rather than netted against sales — they represent returns, not negative demand.
  • Rows with zero/negative quantity or price (data entry errors, non-product entries like postage/bank fees) were removed.
  • ~25% of rows lacked a `CustomerID` (likely guest checkouts); these were kept for revenue-trend analysis but excluded from customer-level work (RFM, cohorts), since they can't be attributed to anyone.
For full methodology you can refer to this notebook : Data cleaning.

Customer Segmentation (RFM)

Customers were scored on Recency, Frequency, and Monetary value (quintiles) and mapped into 9 business-readable segments.
Insight: Revenue concentration here is extreme even by typical retail standards. The Champions segment (recent + frequent buyers) is only 26% of the customer base but drives two-thirds of all revenue. On the other end, 18.9% of customers ("Hibernating/Lost") haven't purchased recently and contribute only 2.1% of revenue — a natural target for a win-back campaign, though the ROI ceiling there is low compared to simply protecting the Champions segment from churn.
For full methodology you can refer to this notebook : RFM Segmentation

Cohort Retention Analysis

Customers were grouped by acquisition month, then tracked for how many stayed active in the following months.
Insight: Retention is both low and largely flat — cohorts don't show a strong "settling in" curve where retention stabilizes after an initial drop-off; the ~21-24% return rate is roughly consistent from month 1 through month 6. Combined with the RFM finding, this reinforces that most customer value comes from a core group who buy repeatedly and often, while the majority of customers churn early. Retention marketing (onboarding sequences, second-purchase incentives) targeted at month 1–2 after first purchase would have the most leverage, since that's where the largest drop-off happens.

Revenue Trend

Seasonality: Revenue climbs steadily from an August low point through a strong November peak (pre-Christmas wholesale ordering), before dropping into a partial/incomplete December (the dataset ends Dec 9, 2011, so that month is not a genuine decline).
Operating pattern: There is essentially zero Saturday activity and revenue is concentrated Monday–Friday during business hours (roughly 9am–4pm). This is a strong signal the customer base is business buyers (florists, gift shops, small retailers restocking), not end consumers — consistent with the large order sizes seen below.
